### [INVESTIGATED, NOT A BUG] RB LightGBM "underprediction" vs Ridge/NN is correct tree-vs-linear conservatism
- **File(s):** Investigation only, no model change. Reproducible analysis now lives in [src/analysis/cohort_analysis.py](../../src/analysis/cohort_analysis.py) (`sparse_history --with-model-error --deep-dive`; legacy wrapper: [src/analysis/analysis_rb_lgbm_disagreement.py](../../src/analysis/analysis_rb_lgbm_disagreement.py)); full write-up [src/analysis/rb_lgbm_disagreement_findings.md](../../src/analysis/rb_lgbm_disagreement_findings.md).
- **What:** Some RB player-weeks show LightGBM far below the other models on the serving UI (flagged case **Derrick Henry 2025 W2** — LGBM ≈ 12.6, NN ≈ 16.3, Attn ≈ 17.2, Ridge ≈ 26.3). Suspected a bug. Confirmed with a class-level comparison against ground truth across all 1643 RB test rows (2025), reusing `result["test_df"]` `pred_{model}_total` columns.
- **Result (real pipeline, 2025 test):** Henry's W2 **actual was 2.3 FP** — he cratered after a 29.2-pt W1 — so LGBM (err +10.3) was the *closest* model and Ridge (+24.0) the worst; his 7.30-FP LGBM-vs-peers gap is the single largest disagreement in the whole test set. Globally LGBM has the **lowest MAE (4.155)** and Ridge (4.514) is **worse than the season-avg baseline (4.310)**. On the exact "LGBM ≪ peers" class (peer-mean − LGBM ≥ 4, n=12) LGBM is most accurate (MAE 7.26, bias −0.16) and Ridge overpredicts (MAE 14.26, bias +8.9); disagreements are 12-to-1 one-directional. Mechanism: at W2 the lagged L3/ewma windows = just the explosive W1, the linear model extrapolates it (esp. rushing TDs: +6.3 of the +9.06 FP gap) while the regularized Huber trees shrink to the base rate. Ridge's high predictions buy **no** ceiling-game accuracy (actual ≥ 20: Ridge MAE 15.23 ≈ LGBM 15.27) — they just land high on mean-reverting weeks.
- **Decision:** No change. This is *why* LightGBM is the production-best model for RB. A Ridge/LGBM blend would re-import Ridge's low-week overprediction variance without ceiling benefit — left as a separately-benchmarkable question, not pursued.
- **Lesson:** "Model A prints lower than Model B" is not a bug report — score both against **ground truth** before assuming the lower one is wrong (the user's example was the closest model on the worst-overprediction week). Tree models structurally cannot extrapolate past training-leaf values; on a volatile mean-reverting target that conservatism is a feature, not a defect. Don't "fix" it by promoting rolling/L3 features into `ATTN_STATIC_FEATURES` or de-regularizing LGBM (both are documented stop-rules). Per-row preds for this kind of slice live on `result["test_df"]`.
- **Attention-NN follow-up (2026-05-30):** Investigated why the *history-aware* attention NN didn't regress to LGBM's ~12 for Henry W2. The within-season sequence (`build_game_history_arrays` groups by `(player_id, season)`) does hold only 1 game, and masking is correct (`AttentionPool` `-inf`-masks padding) — but **attention over a length-1 sequence extracts that game; there is no small-sample → hedge reflex**, and ~17 was well-calibrated (sparse-history rows coming off a hot ≥18-FP game average **16.6 FP** next week; Henry's 2.3 is a 1-of-1 crater). At `n_prior_games==1` Attn (3.68) ≈ LGBM (3.60); the attention NN never has the lowest MAE in *any* history-depth bucket. **One real weak spot — the season opener (`n_prior_games==0`, empty history): Attn over-predicts (bias +1.13, worst non-Ridge) because the history branch is inert and it leans on the static prior.** Spawned a follow-up task to address `npg==0` — **fixed** via a learned no-history embedding (see the `[FIXED] Attention NN over-predicted season openers` entry at the top of this archive).
