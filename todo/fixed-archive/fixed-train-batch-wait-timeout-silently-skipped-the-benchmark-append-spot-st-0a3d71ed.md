### [FIXED] train-batch wait timeout silently skipped the benchmark append (Spot starvation outlasted launch.py's 3h cap)
- **File(s):** [../.github/workflows/train-batch.yml](../../.github/workflows/train-batch.yml) (`timeout-minutes` 210→350, `FF_WAIT_TIMEOUT=18000`, `PYTHONUNBUFFERED`, `continue-on-error` + new `recover` step + explicit step gates + final fail step + `issues: write`), [../src/batch/launch.py](../../src/batch/launch.py) (`FF_BATCH_JOB_IDS_FILE` breadcrumb), tests in [../tests/batch/test_launch_main.py](../../tests/batch/test_launch_main.py); backfilled rows `benchmark_history/2026-06-08T22-48-38_b8d3f3f.json` + `2026-06-10T22-33-22_2fd5449.json`.
- **What:** Runs 27161472255 (b8d3f3f, #1104) and 27299375573 (2fd5449, #1112) failed at almost exactly 3h: the 2026-06-08/06-10 g6 Spot shortage (see the "Batch Spot queue stuck on g6-only capacity" entry below) held jobs RUNNABLE for ~2.7-3h while training itself takes ~1 min/position, so `wait_for_jobs` hit its `WAIT_TIMEOUT_SECONDS=10800` and exited 1 — run 1's six jobs SUCCEEDED 0.5-3.5 **minutes** after the wait expired (AWS `stoppedAt` 22:45:46-22:48:38 vs wait expiry ~22:45:17); run 2 had 5/6 SUCCEEDED in-window and RB (Spot-reclaimed mid-run, attempt 2) SUCCEEDED 29 min late. Every post-step (freshness verify, benchmark append, history push, dedup marker) was skipped by the implicit `if: success()`, so trained artifacts shipped to S3/serving but the MAE/RMSE record silently vanished — including the #1104 baseline for the open QB-inheritance PRs. Two more runs failed identically on 06-08 (fbea9e36 15:31, 2e87dec9 15:32). Forensics were also hampered by Python block-buffering: the entire 3h poll log flushed at exit, stamping every line with the exit time.
- **Fix:** (1) Wait sized to the job's headroom, not an arbitrary 3h: `FF_WAIT_TIMEOUT=18000` (5h) + `timeout-minutes: 350` (budget comment in the workflow) — queue time dominates, so give it the runner. (2) `launch.py` records submitted job ids + expected positions to `FF_BATCH_JOB_IDS_FILE`; a new recovery step on `steps.train.outcome == 'failure'` re-checks those exact jobs (10-min bounded grace poll, position-coverage guard against partial submits) and flips `recovered=true` when all SUCCEEDED — append/push/marker steps gate on `train success || recovered`. (3) Still-incomplete → `::error::` + an auto-filed tracking issue (`issues: write`), and a final step restores the job failure (`continue-on-error` on the wait step keeps the job alive for recovery). (4) `PYTHONUNBUFFERED=1` so the poll log streams live. Backfill: both missing rows were rebuilt from the per-position `benchmark_metrics.json` inside `models/{POS}/history/` tarballs — run 1's were already GC-pruned (`HISTORY_KEEP_N=5`) but recovered via S3 **bucket versioning**; rows written with the production `summarize_pipeline_result`/`append_to_history` path, real completion timestamps, true hardware labels (run 1 actually trained on the g5/A10G fallback), and mirrored to the S3 benchmark prefix.
- **Lesson:** A polling step whose failure mode is "give up while the thing it watches succeeds anyway" must not gate follow-on bookkeeping with implicit step ordering — re-check the watched state before declaring failure, and make the residual failure loud (issue, not just a red run nobody opens; both starvation failures went unnoticed until the missing rows were). Sizing: when queue time dominates execution time, a fixed wait disconnected from the job ceiling just converts headroom into silent loss. And pruned S3 artifacts may still be recoverable: check `get-bucket-versioning` before declaring history gone.
