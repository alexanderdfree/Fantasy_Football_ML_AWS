### [FIXED] Expert prediction-interval bands under-covered — hvpkod NFL.com archive backfills 2021–2023 "projections" with box scores; detect via near-exact fraction, not residual std
- **File(s):** [src/analysis/expert_intervals.py](../../src/analysis/expert_intervals.py) (`_select_fit_seasons` near-exact leak guard `_LOOKAHEAD_EPS=0.5` / `_LOOKAHEAD_FRAC=0.30` + `_STATIONARITY_FRAC=0.8`), committed [src/serving/expert_intervals.json](../../src/serving/expert_intervals.json), serving surface in [src/serving/app.py](../../src/serving/app.py) / templates / `app.js` / `style.css`. Tests: [tests/analysis/test_expert_intervals.py](../../tests/analysis/test_expert_intervals.py) (incl. `test_select_fit_seasons_catches_partial_backfill`), [tests/test_app_comparison.py](../../tests/test_app_comparison.py).
- **What:** The new per-projection intervals (quantile-regress actual on the source's projection, τ=0.1/0.5/0.9) calibrated fine in-sample but **NFL.com offense bands covered only ~0.40 of held-out 2025 actuals** (nominal 0.80). Root cause is in the **third-party hvpkod/NFL-Data archive** (the community scrape of NFL.com — *not* NFL.com itself, which doesn't sneakily backfill): its **2021, 2022 and 2023** NFL.com offense "projected" CSVs contain the *realized box score*, not projections. Proven by an exact-match test (the cleanest evidence, prompted by a reviewer doubting the "backfill" claim): the archive's projected raw passing yards equal the actual ~92–96% of the time for 2021–2023 (e.g. 2023 Josh Allen "projected" 359 / actual 359; RB 2022 22.6→22.6, 10.8→10.8) and are whole integers, whereas 2024–2025 are fractional expected values matching actuals ~0%. The archive evidently switched data source in 2024.
- **Fix (and a fix-of-the-fix):** The *first* attempt keyed the leak guard on per-season residual **std** (drop if std < 2.0). That caught only the fully-copied 2023 (std 0.68) and **missed the partially-backfilled 2021/2022** — a season ~40% backfilled keeps a normal-looking std (~5) because the genuine 60% dominate the variance, so it slipped through and (for RB) got pooled into the fit, leaving RB at 0.72. The correct detector is the **near-exact fraction**: drop a season if >`_LOOKAHEAD_FRAC` (0.30) of rows land within `_LOOKAHEAD_EPS` (0.5 pt) of the actual — the copied rows pile up at residual≈0 regardless of overall std. On the gsis panel genuine cells sit ≤0.18 (NFL.com 2024/25, NFL.com K, all RotoWire) while every backfilled NFL.com offense cell is ≥0.40, so 0.30 separates with margin. Result: NFL.com offense fits on its only genuine pre-2025 season (2024) — RB 0.72→**0.78** — while NFL.com K and all RotoWire keep their full history; every cell 0.72–0.82. `test_committed_expert_intervals_contract` pins per-cell coverage in CI.
- **Lesson:** Two lessons. (1) **Direction:** the brief warned *RotoWire* might be look-ahead; the empirics said the opposite — RotoWire is clean every year and **NFL.com (via the community archive)** was contaminated. Don't trust a provenance caveat's direction; measure. (2) **Detector:** residual std (or any aggregate-spread metric) only catches *fully* copied seasons — **partial backfill hides in a normal std**. The right test for "is this projection secretly the actual?" is the **exact/near-exact match fraction to the realized value**, validated on a genuinely held-out slice. Integer-vs-fractional is a corroborating tell (real projections are fractional expected values). A reviewer's "I don't believe that" is what forced the forensic per-row check that found 2021/2022 too — cheap-proxy diagnostics (std) can be directionally right but quantitatively incomplete.
