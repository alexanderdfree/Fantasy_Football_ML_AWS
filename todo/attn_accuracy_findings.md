## TL;DR

- **Overall the four models are nearly tied**: LightGBM 4.142 ≈ **ATTN 4.158** <
  NN 4.198 < Ridge 4.307 (pooled MAE). ATTN trails the best model by 0.016 pts —
  inside seed noise. *Do not* headline an overall winner from this gap.
- **The robust signal is per-position direction (holds across both seeds):**
  ATTN is the **best model on DST** and the **worst-of-the-learned-models on K**;
  it loses by a hair on QB/RB/TE; WR is a coin-flip (the "win" flips by seed).
- **The dominant error structure is regression-to-the-mean, identical in shape
  for every model**: over-predict the lowest-scoring quartile, badly
  under-predict ceiling games. ATTN is the **best-calibrated learned model at the
  high tail** (least under-shoot on Q4).

**Interpretation.** ATTN's edge tracks how much exploitable *per-game temporal

learned model, and ATTN's sequence machinery is pure overhead there. On **QB**,
LightGBM's rolling-stat trees edge ATTN on the high-magnitude `passing_yards`
head. RB/TE/WR are effectively a wash with LightGBM.

## 2. Week-by-week

- ATTN beats its best peer in only **7/18** weeks, and the per-week gaps are tiny
  (|Δ| < 0.09 pts). The four models' weekly MAE curves move together — **no model
  owns a particular stretch of the season.**
- MAE is **lowest in Week 1** (~3.9) and peaks **mid-season (W7–8 ≈ 4.45)** and
  **W15 (≈4.4)** — opposite to the "cold-start week 1 is hardest" intuition; the
  hard weeks are the high-variance mid/late slates, not the data-starved opener.
- **Systematic under-prediction every week.** NN, ATTN and LightGBM all carry
  negative bias (−0.7 to −1.5 pts) in *every* week (worst W5, W15); **Ridge is
  near-unbiased** (−0.0 to −0.7). The learned/tree models shrink toward the mean
  harder than Ridge — the price of their lower variance.

## 2b. Week-by-week vs the experts (NFL.com + Sleeper)

`--experts` overlays NFL.com and Sleeper (RotoWire) projections on the weekly
chart. Both are re-scored to the **same PPR `fantasy_points`** the models target
(via `predictions_to_fantasy_points`) and inherit the loaders' placeholder
filtering, so a matched row is a *genuine* projection, not a 0.0 stand-in.
Coverage differs — **NFL.com has no DST, Sleeper has no K** — so the only fair
single all-lines-comparable chart is the **offense skill positions (QB/RB/WR/TE)**

**Every line is recomputed on that identical subset**, so its MAE runs higher
than §1's full-population numbers (experts only cover high-volume startable

## 3. Subgroup: actual-score tier (judged by signed bias)

On skewed FP targets low-scoring rows have lower MAE regardless of model quality,
so "is the model worse on X?" is a **bias** question. Signed bias = mean(pred −
actual); + = over-predict.

| tier (per-position quartile) | Ridge | NN | **Attention NN** | LightGBM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (lowest actual) | +3.468 | +2.828 | **+2.664** | +2.902 |
| Q2 | +2.488 | +1.601 | **+1.572** | +1.822 |
| Q3 | +0.191 | −0.807 | **−0.692** | −0.613 |
| Q4 (ceiling games) | −6.982 | −7.971 | **−7.619** | −7.936 |

**Every model regresses hard to the mean**: it inflates scrub weeks (+2.7 to
+3.5) and massively under-calls ceiling weeks (−7 to −8). This single bias axis
dwarfs every model-vs-model MAE difference above. Among the **learned** models,
**ATTN is the best-calibrated at both tails** (smallest Q1 over-shoot and
smallest Q4 under-shoot) — its attention pooling reaches the boom games slightly
better than NN/LightGBM. Ridge looks best on Q4 only because it under-shrinks
everywhere (and pays for it with the worst Q1 over-prediction).

## 4. Subgroup: season phase × position

ATTN's relative standing shifts with how much in-season history has accumulated:

- **K**: ATTN's loss to Ridge **widens late** (early +0.19 → late **+0.38**) —
  more kicking history does not help the sequence model.
- **DST**: ATTN's win **grows late** (early −0.06 → late **−0.16**) — defensive
  momentum is most learnable once a sequence exists.
- **RB**: ATTN wins **early** (−0.07) but loses mid/late to LightGBM.
- **WR**: ATTN wins mid/late, loses early — opposite of RB, and small enough to
  be the same seed-level noise that flips its overall result.

## Method notes / caveats

- **Multi-seed was load-bearing.** A single seed (or single position) would have
  reported an ATTN WR "win" and a QB-specific "most-extreme tail bias" — both
  evaporate when pooled across seed 42 + 123. Per the project lesson, single-seed
  NN overall-MAE is noise; the trustworthy claims here are the *direction*
  signals stable across both seeds (DST win, K loss) and the *bias* structure.
- Overall MAE deltas < ~0.05 pt between the top models are within seed spread —
  treat ATTN/LightGBM/NN as a three-way tie overall and decide per-position.
- Predictions are the production pipeline's per-row `result["test_df"]`
  (`pred_{model}_total`); no models were reimplemented.

## Actionable leads (not changes — analysis only)

1. **K should not use the attention NN as its headline model** — Ridge wins
   cleanly and robustly; the sequence model is overhead on kicking.
2. **The universal regression-to-mean (Q4 −7 to −8) is the single biggest error
   source**, far larger than any model choice. A ceiling-aware loss head or a
   quantile/upside calibration would move overall accuracy more than swapping
   models. Tune per the loss-weight↔δ coupling (`1/δ` for the MSE yards heads
   since PR #870), not independently.
3. **DST is where attention genuinely pays off** — a candidate position for any
   future attention-architecture investment.
