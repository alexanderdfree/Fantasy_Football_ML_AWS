# ADR-0001: Temporal split (2012–23 train / 2024 val / 2025 test)

**Status:** Accepted

**Decision.** Split by season, not by random row. Train on 2012–2023, validate on 2024, hold out 2025 for test.

**Context.** Weekly fantasy data is a time series with heavy week-over-week autocorrelation within a player (rolling features by construction). A random train/test split would leak week W+1's rolling stats into week W's label.

**Options considered.**

| Option | Complexity | Leakage risk | Data-efficiency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Random row split | Low | **High** (rolling features leak) | High |
| K-fold time-series CV | Medium | Low | Medium |
| Single season-based holdout (chosen) | Low | Low | Medium |

**Chosen: single season holdout.** Matches how the model will actually be used (next season is unknown; last season is the fairest holdout). Ridge hyperparameter tuning still uses expanding-window CV *inside* the 2012–2023 train window to avoid a single fold's noise — so we get some of the statistical benefit of K-fold without contaminating the test year.

**Rejected.** K-fold over seasons was considered but over-spent our limited post-2012 history and broke the "deployment mirror" intuition — at serve time we're always predicting a future we've never trained on, and a single holdout faithfully simulates that.

**Extension (2026-05-29) — rolling-origin reporting.** The single holdout makes every headline metric an `n=1`-season point estimate (a 0.02 MAE gap between two models is indistinguishable from season noise). `rolling_origin_folds` ([src/data/split.py](../../src/data/split.py)) + `benchmark.py --rolling-origin` optionally score N forward holdouts (`ROLLING_ORIGIN_TEST_SEASONS=[2023,2024,2025]`; per origin train `[..T-2]` / val `T-1` / test `T`) and report per-model MAE/R²/top-12 as **mean±std**. This is the walk-forward extension of the same expanding-window principle already used for Ridge tuning — **not** the K-fold rejected above: every origin still trains strictly on the past, and the final origin (test 2025) reproduces this split season-for-season. It is complemented by a *within-season* paired bootstrap ([src/analysis/significance.py](../../src/analysis/significance.py)) that answers the orthogonal question — sampling noise within one season vs. variance across seasons. Headline/operator-invoked only (~N× cost); the per-PR benchmark stays single-split. As of 2026-06-01, benchmark-history publishing CLIs treat `--cv` as a deprecated alias for this rolling-origin report; direct `run_cv_pipeline` / per-position `run_cv()` remain available for ad-hoc diagnostics and tuner-style validation objectives.

**References.** [src/data/split.py:6-37](../../src/data/split.py), season constants in [src/config.py](../../src/config.py). Knowledge cutoff to 2012 landed in commit `f400a5c`.

- **2026-05-30** — Documented the **2021 16→17 game schedule expansion** as a known, contained covariate shift (analysis only — no code change, no retrain). The NFL went from 16 games / 17 weeks (≤2020) to 17 games / 18 weeks (2021+); `TRAIN_SEASONS=2012–2023` ([src/config.py](../../src/config.py)) therefore pools both regimes while `VAL=2024`/`TEST=2025` are 17-game — so the 17-game era is **trained and tested, not extrapolated**. The design contains the break almost everywhere: targets are **per-game raw stats** (game-count-invariant — the primary protection); rolling/EWMA/trend/share features **reset per season** (`groupby(["player_id","season"])`, [src/features/engineer.py](../../src/features/engineer.py)) so they are not rescaled; the attention history sequence is indexed by **games** with `attn_max_seq_len=17`, which exactly fits a 17-game season (intentional — *not* a truncation bug; a team plays 17 games across 18 weeks, so "18 weeks" ≠ "18 games"); and `week` (now 1–18) is already represented in TRAIN via 2021–2023, so week-18 is not novel at inference. The lone real leak is the `prior_season_total_*` features (`prior_season_total_yards` / `_touchdowns` / `_redzone_touches`, full-season **sums** in [src/features/engineer.py](../../src/features/engineer.py)), which carry a ~6.25% (17/16) season-length inflation at equal per-game productivity — partially self-mitigated by the per-game-mean prior aggregates beside them and the `prior_season_games_played` active-games normaliser, and small next to the injury-driven games-played variance that already exists. No season-total *projection* exists in the targets/backtest, so there is no hidden hardcoded-16 bug. **Verdict:** minor and contained; the totals are **kept by design** (they carry availability/volume signal the per-game means don't) — revisit them with a measured counterfactual diagnostic (e.g. via [src/analysis/covariate_shift.py](../../src/analysis/covariate_shift.py)) only if a future benchmark regression points there, and revisit `attn_max_seq_len=17` only if the NFL moves to 18 games. Analysis only, no decision changed; relates to **D1** (the temporal split pools the two regimes) and the eval-methodology covariate-shift guard, and is topically adjacent to the late-season wk17/18 rest-effect analysis ([#608](https://github.com/alexanderdfree/Fantasy_Football_ML_AWS/pull/608)). No [todo/fixed-archive.md](../../todo/fixed-archive.md) entry (no bug fixed).
